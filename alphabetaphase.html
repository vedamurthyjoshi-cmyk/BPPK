Alpha and Beta Phases in Two-Compartment Model

In a two-compartment model, the semi-log Cp-time plot shows two distinct phases after IV bolus — a rapid α-phase and a slower β-phase.

Alpha (α) Phase — Distribution Phase

Initial rapid decline of plasma concentration. Drug rapidly distributes from central compartment (plasma, well-perfused organs) to peripheral compartment (muscle, fat, slowly perfused tissues). Rate constant α is large (fast process).

Beta (β) Phase — Elimination Phase

Slower terminal decline. Drug has equilibrated between compartments. Elimination from central compartment dominates. The slope of the terminal log-linear phase = -β/2.303. β < α always.

Feathering Method

To resolve α and β: extrapolate terminal β line to Y-axis → get B and β. Subtract extrapolated values from observed Cp → residuals = A·e^(-αt). Plot residuals → straight line → get A and α.

Clinical Drugs with Two-Compartment Behaviour

Digoxin, aminoglycosides (gentamicin, tobramycin), lidocaine, cyclosporine, thiopental, heparin. Important for aminoglycoside TDM: sample at α-phase equilibration for trough, not immediately post-dose.
