First Pass Effect

After oral administration, drugs absorbed from the GIT enter the portal circulation and pass through the liver before reaching systemic circulation. The liver may extensively metabolise the drug, significantly reducing its bioavailability. This is the first pass effect (presystemic or pre-systemic metabolism).

Clinical Significance

Drugs with high first-pass effect have low oral bioavailability and require much higher oral doses than parenteral doses. Examples: morphine (oral BA ~30%), lignocaine (oral BA ~35%), propranolol (oral BA ~26%), nitroglycerin (oral BA ~1% — given sublingually to bypass liver).

Bypassing First Pass Effect

Sublingual (nitroglycerin), transdermal (fentanyl patches), rectal (suppositories — partial bypass), inhalation, and parenteral routes all bypass hepatic first-pass metabolism. Prodrugs can also be used to improve oral BA.
