GIT Anatomy and Drug Absorption

The gastrointestinal tract is the most common route for drug administration. Understanding its anatomy is essential to predicting drug absorption.

Stomach (pH 1.5–3.5)

Weak acids (aspirin, ibuprofen) are un-ionised at low pH and can be absorbed here. However, limited surface area means most absorption occurs downstream. Delayed gastric emptying slows drug delivery to small intestine.

Small Intestine (pH 6–7.4)

The primary absorption site due to its enormous surface area (~200 m² with villi and microvilli). Drugs cross the enterocyte membrane by passive diffusion, active transport, or pinocytosis. The enterocytes also contain CYP3A4 — contributing to intestinal first-pass metabolism.

Portal Circulation and Hepatic First-Pass Effect

Absorbed drug enters the portal vein → liver. Hepatic enzymes (CYP450) metabolise a fraction before it reaches systemic circulation. High extraction ratio drugs (e.g., morphine, propranolol) have very low oral bioavailability due to extensive first-pass effect.

Colon and Rectum

Limited absorption surface. Suppositories administered rectally partially bypass hepatic first-pass (lower rectal veins drain to systemic, upper to portal).
