Oral Absorption — From Tablet to Systemic Circulation

After oral dosing, a drug undergoes a series of sequential processes before reaching the systemic circulation. Each step can reduce the amount of drug that finally reaches the target.

Step 1 — Disintegration and Dissolution

Tablet disintegrates → drug particles dissolve in GI fluid. Rate governed by drug physicochemical properties, particle size, and formulation. Rate-limiting for BCS Class II drugs.

Step 2 — Absorption across GI Wall

Dissolved drug crosses enterocytes — primarily passive diffusion for lipophilic drugs, carrier-mediated for hydrophilic drugs. P-gp efflux may reduce absorption. Gut wall CYP3A4 contributes to first-pass metabolism.

Step 3 — Portal Vein Transport

Absorbed drug enters portal vein and is carried directly to the liver. The liver extracts a fraction of the drug before it reaches systemic circulation — the first-pass effect. Extraction ratio (ER) determines the fraction lost.

Step 4 — Systemic Circulation

F (bioavailability) = Fa × Fg × Fh. Fa = fraction absorbed from gut, Fg = fraction surviving gut wall metabolism, Fh = fraction surviving hepatic first-pass. All three factors reduce F.
