Passive Diffusion & pH Partition Theory

Passive diffusion is the primary mechanism of drug absorption across biological membranes. It requires no energy and moves drug from high to low concentration.

Fick's Law of Diffusion

Rate = D × A × (C₁ − C₂) / h. Where D = diffusion coefficient, A = surface area, C₁−C₂ = concentration gradient, h = membrane thickness.

pH Partition Theory

Only the un-ionised form of a drug crosses lipid membranes. Weak acids are less ionised at low pH (acidic environment) and more ionised at high pH. Weak bases are the opposite. The Henderson-Hasselbalch equation predicts the degree of ionisation at any given pH.

Henderson-Hasselbalch Equations

Weak acids: pH = pKa + log([A⁻]/[HA]). Weak bases: pH = pKa + log([B]/[BH⁺]). Clinical implication: aspirin (weak acid, pKa 3.5) is well absorbed in the acidic stomach. Morphine (weak base, pKa 8) is better absorbed in the alkaline intestine.
