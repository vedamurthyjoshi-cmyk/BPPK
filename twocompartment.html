Two-Compartment Open Model

When plasma concentration-time data after IV bolus shows a biexponential decline (two distinct phases), a two-compartment model applies. The body is divided into a central compartment (plasma + well-perfused tissues) and a peripheral compartment (muscle, fat, skin).

Biexponential Equation

Cp = A·e^(-α·t) + B·e^(-β·t). Alpha (α) phase: rapid distribution from central to peripheral compartment. Beta (β) phase: slower elimination phase once distribution equilibrium is reached. t½α = 0.693/α, t½β = 0.693/β.

Feathering (Residuals) Method

Semi-log plot shows biphasic decline. Extrapolate terminal line to get B and β. Subtract to get residuals — gives A and α. Used to estimate all four parameters (A, B, α, β).
