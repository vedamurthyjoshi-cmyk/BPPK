Rate Processes in Pharmacokinetics

The rate at which a drug is absorbed, distributed, metabolised, or eliminated follows mathematical rate laws. The two most important are zero order and first order.

Zero Order Kinetics

Drug amount decreases at a CONSTANT RATE regardless of concentration. Rate = k₀ (constant). Graph: straight line decline. Example: alcohol elimination, some controlled-release formulations, IV infusion at fixed rate. Equation: C = C₀ − k₀t

First Order Kinetics

Drug amount decreases at a rate PROPORTIONAL to the amount present. Rate = k·C. As drug level falls, the rate of elimination also falls. Graph: curved decline (exponential). Semi-log plot: straight line. Example: most drug absorptions and eliminations. Equation: C = C₀·e^(−kt)

Half-life (t½)

First order: t½ = 0.693/k — CONSTANT regardless of dose. Zero order: t½ = C₀/2k₀ — DEPENDS on initial concentration. This distinction is clinically critical.
