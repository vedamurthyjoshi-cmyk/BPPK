Biopharmaceutics Classification System (BCS)

BCS classifies drugs by aqueous solubility and intestinal permeability. Proposed by Amidon et al. (1995). Used by FDA, EMA, WHO to grant dissolution biowaivers for generic drugs.

Class I — High Solubility, High Permeability

Well-absorbed. Rate-limiting step is gastric emptying. Biowaiver possible if rapid dissolution (>85% in 15 min). Examples: metoprolol, paracetamol, verapamil, propranolol.

Class II — Low Solubility, High Permeability

Dissolution-limited absorption. Formulation critical — particle size reduction, solid dispersions, lipid formulations. ~70% of new chemical entities are Class II. Examples: ibuprofen, phenytoin, griseofulvin, ketoconazole.

Class III — High Solubility, Low Permeability

Membrane permeation rate-limiting. P-gp efflux may contribute. Biowaiver possible if formulation has no permeation-altering excipients. Examples: atenolol, cimetidine, acyclovir, ranitidine.

Class IV — Low Solubility, Low Permeability

Both barriers present. Variable, poor bioavailability. Difficult to formulate. Examples: furosemide, hydrochlorothiazide, paclitaxel (taxol).
