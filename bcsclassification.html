BCS Classification System

The BCS classifies drugs into 4 classes based on aqueous solubility and intestinal permeability. Proposed by Amidon et al. (1995), it predicts oral absorption and guides formulation strategy.

Class I — High Solubility, High Permeability

Well absorbed. Bioavailability is formulation-independent. Eligible for biowaiver. Examples: metformin, propranolol.

Class II — Low Solubility, High Permeability

Absorption is dissolution rate-limited. Nanoparticles, solid dispersions used to improve BA. Examples: ibuprofen, carbamazepine, griseofulvin.

Class III — High Solubility, Low Permeability

Absorption is permeability-limited. Good dissolution but poor membrane crossing. Examples: ranitidine, atenolol, acyclovir.

Class IV — Low Solubility, Low Permeability

Poor oral bioavailability. Problematic class. Examples: furosemide, ritonavir.
