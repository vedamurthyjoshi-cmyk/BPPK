Active Transport & P-glycoprotein

Not all drug absorption is passive. Several carrier-mediated processes either facilitate or oppose drug absorption.

Active Transport (Influx)

Drugs that resemble endogenous substrates (amino acids, sugars, vitamins) are carried into the cell by specific membrane transporters. Requires energy (ATP). Can be saturated at high drug concentrations. Examples: levodopa (amino acid transporter), vitamin B12 (intrinsic factor-mediated).

P-glycoprotein (P-gp) — Efflux Transporter

P-gp is an efflux pump that removes drugs from the enterocyte back into the intestinal lumen, reducing absorption. It is encoded by the MDR1 (ABCB1) gene. Found in intestinal epithelium, blood-brain barrier, liver, kidney. Drugs that are P-gp substrates have lower bioavailability. P-gp inhibitors (ritonavir, verapamil) increase bioavailability of co-administered P-gp substrates. P-gp inducers (rifampicin) reduce bioavailability.
