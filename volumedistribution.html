Volume of Distribution (Vd)

Vd is an apparent volume relating the total amount of drug in the body to the plasma concentration. Vd = Dose / Cp₀. It is not a real physiological volume.

Interpretation by Size

Small Vd (~5 L): Drug confined to plasma — high molecular weight, highly protein-bound (heparin, warfarin). Moderate Vd (~14–42 L): Distributes into body water (aspirin, theophylline). Large Vd (>100 L): Extensive tissue binding — lipophilic drugs (chloroquine ~250 L/kg, digoxin ~600 L, amiodarone ~5000 L).

Key Relationship

t½ = 0.693 × Vd / CL. Large Vd → long half-life. Loading dose = Css × Vd. Large Vd drugs cannot be removed by dialysis.

Factors Determining Vd

Lipophilicity (↑lipophilicity → ↑Vd). Plasma protein binding (↑PPB → ↓Vd). Tissue binding (↑tissue binding → ↑Vd). Ionisation at pH 7.4.
