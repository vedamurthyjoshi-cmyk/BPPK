P-glycoprotein (P-gp) and Drug Efflux

P-glycoprotein (P-gp, MDR1) is an ATP-dependent efflux transporter located on the apical surface of intestinal enterocytes. It actively pumps drugs that have entered the enterocyte back into the gut lumen, significantly reducing their bioavailability.

Mechanism

P-gp binds lipophilic substrates within the cell membrane and uses ATP hydrolysis to expel them against their concentration gradient, back into the intestinal lumen. This creates a futile cycle where the drug is absorbed and immediately pumped back out.

Clinical Significance

Many important drugs are P-gp substrates: digoxin, cyclosporine, paclitaxel, HIV protease inhibitors, and many anticancer agents. Their low oral bioavailability is partly due to P-gp efflux.

Drug Interactions

P-gp inhibitors (grapefruit juice, verapamil, ketoconazole, ritonavir) block efflux, dramatically increasing bioavailability of substrates. Example: quinidine inhibits P-gp → digoxin levels double. Rifampicin induces P-gp → reduced cyclosporine levels → risk of transplant rejection.

Pharmacogenomics

MDR1 gene polymorphisms affect P-gp expression — explaining inter-individual variation in drug response and bioavailability.
