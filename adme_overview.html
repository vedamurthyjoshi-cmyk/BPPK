ADME — The Four Processes of Drug Fate

After a drug is administered, it undergoes four sequential processes collectively called ADME. The plasma concentration-time profile of any drug is the net result of all four processes occurring simultaneously.

A — Absorption

Movement of drug from the site of administration into the systemic circulation. Rate and extent depend on drug physicochemical properties, formulation, and physiological factors. Oral absorption involves dissolution, GI transit, and membrane permeation.

D — Distribution

Drug leaving blood and entering tissues. Determined by plasma protein binding, tissue binding, lipophilicity, and blood flow. Described quantitatively by Volume of Distribution (Vd).

M — Metabolism

Biotransformation of drug, primarily in liver. Phase I (CYP450 — oxidation, reduction, hydrolysis) and Phase II (conjugation). May produce active, inactive, or toxic metabolites.

E — Elimination

Removal of drug from body — via renal excretion (unchanged drug or metabolites) and biliary excretion. Described by clearance (CL) and half-life (t½).
