Drug Metabolism — Hepatic Biotransformation

Metabolism converts lipophilic drugs to polar, water-soluble metabolites for excretion. The liver is the primary site, via CYP450 enzymes (Phase I) and conjugation enzymes (Phase II).

Phase I — Functionalisation (CYP450)

Oxidation (most common), reduction, hydrolysis. Introduces or unmasks polar functional groups (-OH, -NH₂, -SH). Products may be active (codeine→morphine), inactive, or toxic (paracetamol→NAPQI). Key enzymes: CYP3A4 (~50% of drugs), CYP2D6, CYP2C9, CYP1A2.

Phase II — Conjugation

Glucuronidation (most common), sulfation, acetylation, methylation, glutathione conjugation. Products are highly polar, pharmacologically inactive, and readily excreted in urine or bile.

Drug Interactions

Inducers (rifampicin, phenobarbitone, carbamazepine): increase CYP expression → faster metabolism → lower drug levels. Inhibitors (fluconazole, ciprofloxacin, grapefruit): decrease CYP activity → slower metabolism → higher drug levels.
