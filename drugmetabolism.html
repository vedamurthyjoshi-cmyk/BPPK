Drug Metabolism — Cytochrome P450 (CYP450)

Drug metabolism is the enzymatic biotransformation of a drug into a different chemical form (metabolite). The liver is the primary site. The goal is to convert lipophilic drugs into more water-soluble forms for excretion.

Phase I Reactions

Introduce or unmask a polar functional group. Reactions: oxidation (most common, by CYP450), reduction, hydrolysis. CYP3A4 metabolises ~50% of all drugs. CYP2D6 (~25%), CYP2C9 (~15%), CYP1A2 (~10%). Genetic polymorphism: poor metabolisers vs ultra-rapid metabolisers.

Phase II Reactions (Conjugation)

Attach a polar endogenous molecule. Reactions: glucuronidation (UGT enzymes), sulfation, acetylation, methylation, glutathione conjugation. Products are almost always inactive and water-soluble.

Drug Interactions at CYP450

Inducers (rifampicin, carbamazepine, phenytoin): increase CYP activity → faster metabolism → reduced drug effect. Inhibitors (ketoconazole, fluconazole, erythromycin): decrease CYP activity → slower metabolism → drug accumulation → toxicity.
