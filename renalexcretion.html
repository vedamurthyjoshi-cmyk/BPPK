Renal Drug Excretion

Renal excretion involves three processes: glomerular filtration (passive), tubular secretion (active), and tubular reabsorption (passive). Net renal clearance = filtration + secretion − reabsorption.

Glomerular Filtration

GFR ~125 mL/min in a healthy adult. Only the free (unbound) fraction of drug is filtered. CL(filtration) = GFR × fu. Reduced in renal failure — dose adjustment required for aminoglycosides, vancomycin, digoxin, metformin.

Tubular Secretion

Active transport by OAT (organic anion transporters) and OCT (cation transporters) in proximal tubule. Can secrete protein-bound drug. Saturable — drug interactions (probenecid blocks penicillin secretion). CLr > GFR×fu indicates active secretion.

Tubular Reabsorption

Passive back-diffusion of lipophilic, non-ionised drug from tubular lumen into blood. Urine pH manipulation: alkalinising urine (NaHCO₃) traps weak acids, increasing excretion. Used in salicylate and phenobarbitone overdose.
