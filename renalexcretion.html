Renal Excretion of Drugs

The kidney is the primary route of drug elimination for water-soluble drugs and metabolites. Three processes operate simultaneously in the nephron.

1. Glomerular Filtration

Passive, non-selective ultrafiltration through the glomerular capillary. Only free (unbound) drug is filtered — protein-bound drug is NOT filtered. GFR in healthy adults ≈ 125 mL/min. Creatinine clearance is used as a surrogate for GFR in dose adjustment.

2. Active Tubular Secretion

Active transport of drug from peritubular blood into the tubular lumen against a concentration gradient. Two transporter systems: organic acid transporter (OAT) for acidic drugs (penicillin, furosemide), organic cation transporter (OCT) for basic drugs (metformin, cimetidine). Can be saturated at high doses — zero order at saturation.

3. Tubular Reabsorption

Passive back-diffusion of un-ionised, lipophilic drug from tubular lumen back into blood. Urine pH manipulation changes ionisation → affects reabsorption. Alkaline urine (NaHCO₃): traps acidic drugs in urine → increased excretion. Acidic urine (NH₄Cl): traps basic drugs → increased excretion. Used clinically in drug overdose management.
